# Item selection from the dev split

An item on which every evaluated system gets the same outcome adds the same amount to every system's score and separates nothing. This page gives, per family, how many items do separate the systems, and a smaller subset chosen on the `dev` split by a fixed rule that keeps those items. The `test` split is never read to choose an item.

Selection runs: `results/reference`, layer `quick`, benchmark 0.6.0, 5 systems (claude-sonnet-5, deepseek-v4-pro-0813, gemini-3.8-flash, gpt-5.6-terra, llama-4-maverick), 319 dev items, 4000 permutations. Generated by `python tools/item_select.py --runs results/reference --out docs/ITEM_SELECTION.md`.

## Per-item statistics

| statistic | definition |
|---|---|
| constant on primary | the family's primary outcome (the one `keystone.metrics.PRIMARY_OUTCOME` names for it) is the same for every system |
| constant on paired | edited forbidden minus paraphrase forbidden is the same for every system |
| item-rest r | Pearson correlation over systems between the item's primary outcome and the system's mean primary outcome on the other items of the family |
| ceiling | share of systems whose reply to the unedited conversation already takes a forbidden action |
| instability | share of systems whose forbidden verdict differs between the unedited conversation and its paraphrase-only control |

## Rule

A perturbation-family item is kept when it is not constant on both outcomes, its item-rest r is not negative, its ceiling is at most 0.50 and its instability at most 0.25. A family left with fewer than 20 items, the smallest family `tools/discrimination.py` reports, is filled in a fixed order: informative items first, then by item-rest r, then by the cleaner measurement, then by the build's hash order.

The two negative controls are not selected on outcomes, since their prediction is that systems do not differ and choosing the items on which they do would manufacture the opposite. They pass the ceiling and instability checks and are then taken in hash order, in the number that keeps their share of the subset at their share of the full set (0.25).

## Transfer to the held-out split

Dev and test are split by source, so no item chosen above appears on `test`. The rule is carried over by the annotation fields every twin has, `evidence_state`, `group`, `turns`, `materiality_majority`, `state_review_behaviour_agree`: on dev, each value of each field gets the keep rate of the items carrying it, shrunk toward the family's keep rate with a prior weight of 10 items; a held-out item scores the mean over its fields, and each family keeps its highest-scoring items at its dev keep rate. The negative controls are taken in hash order at the same share as above. Applied back to the dev items it was fitted on, the transfer rule agrees with the outcome rule on 21 of 25 `missing_evidence` items (chance 15.6), 14 of 21 `conflicting_evidence` items (chance 11.0), 16 of 23 `buried_red_flag` items (chance 13.2), 16 of 20 `demographic_shift` items (chance 10.0), 15 of 20 `alternative_evidence` items (chance 10.0), 17 of 22 `missing_evidence_early` items (chance 12.4). That agreement is in sample; given held-out runs (`--eval-runs`), the tool scores the transferred subset on the `test` split in the table below it.
